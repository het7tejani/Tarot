Archangel names commonly encountered in religious and spiritual writing include Michael, Gabriel, Raphael and Uriel. Lists of seven also circulate, but there is no single list shared by every tradition. A useful guide names the text or community behind a list instead of presenting all angel writing as one system.

An archangel is generally understood as a chief or leading angel. Descriptions of these figures belong to religious traditions and beliefs. Learning their stories does not require treating a repeated number, dream or coincidence as proof that an angel has contacted you.

## Michael: the warrior figure

Michael is often depicted in religious art as a warrior, sometimes carrying a sword or overcoming a dragon. Encyclopaedia Britannica describes him as a leader of the heavenly hosts. His name and stories occur in more than one Abrahamic religious tradition, though the accounts are not identical.

In modern spiritual reflection, people sometimes connect Michael with courage or protection. Keep the distinction clear: a devotional association is not a guarantee of physical safety. If you are facing an actual threat, act on the situation and seek appropriate help.

A practical reflection might be to identify a boundary you need to communicate. You do not have to believe that an outside force chose the boundary for you. Decide what you can honestly maintain and how to express it without making a promise you cannot keep.

## Gabriel: the messenger

Gabriel is widely associated with messages and announcements. In the Book of Daniel, Gabriel explains a vision. In Christian scripture, Gabriel appears in the Annunciation narrative. Gabriel is also known in Islam as Jibril, with spelling varying in English transliteration.

Those are related religious histories, not interchangeable accounts. If you are studying a particular faith, read its own sources and guidance rather than relying on a general spiritual keyword list.

For personal reflection, the messenger theme can become a question about communication. What needs to be said plainly? What information do you still need? An interpretation should not make you mistake an assumption for a message from another person.

## Raphael: the Tobit story

Raphael is a central figure in the Book of Tobit, accompanying Tobias on his journey while initially disguised as a human. Britannica also discusses Raphael in the First Book of Enoch. These textual settings matter because different communities give these books different standing.

Raphael is commonly associated with healing in religious and spiritual writing. That association does not establish that prayer, an angel reading or a purchased object can treat an illness. Spiritual practice can sit alongside care, but it should not delay medical help or replace a clinician's advice.

If this story interests you, begin with the text and its context. You can reflect on accepting support or being a helpful companion without assigning a supernatural cause to a health change.

## Uriel: light and interpretation

Uriel appears in Jewish and Christian apocryphal and related writings. Britannica gives the name's meaning as "fire of God" or "light of God" and describes several different associations across texts. This is a useful example of why one name need not have one fixed role.

Modern guides often connect Uriel with understanding or insight. Treat that as an interpretive use. An idea still needs checking, even if it arrives during prayer or a moment that feels meaningful.

You could write down the question you are trying to understand and the evidence you have. Then identify what remains unknown. That is different from assuming a feeling of certainty proves the answer.

## What are the seven archangel names?

One list associated with the First Book of Enoch includes Uriel, Raphael, Raguel, Michael, Sariel, Gabriel and Remiel. Spellings can vary between translations. Other religious and modern spiritual lists use different names, so seeing a different list is a reason to check its source.

Avoid turning a list from one text into a claim that every religion teaches the same seven figures. The number seven and the names used need their own context. When comparing guides, ask which book or tradition each writer is discussing.

This also separates archangel names from contemporary [angel-number interpretations](/blog/333-angel-number-meaning). A numerology guide and an account of a religious text are different kinds of material. One does not authenticate the other.

## Using the names respectfully

If these figures belong to your faith, your community's teaching is a better guide to devotional practice than a generic list. If you are reading out of curiosity, keep the original context attached to the story.

You do not need to buy a reading or identify a personal archangel to learn about the subject. Be cautious of claims that you must pay immediately to remove a threat or secure protection. No name on this page verifies such a claim.

## Common questions

**Are there always seven archangels?**

No single number or list is universal across every tradition. Name the source when using a list of seven.

**Are angel numbers messages from archangels?**

Some people interpret them that way as a belief. Seeing a number cannot verify who, if anyone, sent a message.

**Can an archangel name reveal my future?**

A name or a reading cannot establish a future event. Keep important decisions grounded in evidence and your own consent.
