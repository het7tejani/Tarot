A standard tarot deck has 78 cards: 22 Major Arcana and 56 Minor Arcana. The Minor Arcana divides into four suits of 14 cards each. In a Rider-Waite-Smith-style deck, those suits are Wands, Cups, Swords and Pentacles.

That gives you a straightforward count: 22 + 56 = 78. If your new deck contains more cards, check its booklet before assuming something is wrong. Publishers sometimes include an extra illustrated card, a title card or a reference card. Those additions are separate from the standard structure.

## The 22 Major Arcana cards

The Major Arcana is the group containing cards such as the Fool, the Lovers and the World. In many modern decks, the Fool is numbered 0 and the remaining cards run from I through XXI. Zero is still one card, so this sequence contains 22 cards rather than 21.

The standard names are the Fool, the Magician, the High Priestess, the Empress, the Emperor, the Hierophant, the Lovers, the Chariot, Strength, the Hermit, Wheel of Fortune, Justice, the Hanged Man, Death, Temperance, the Devil, the Tower, the Star, the Moon, the Sun, Judgement and the World.

Names and numbering can vary by tradition. Strength and Justice are a familiar example: their positions differ between some decks. Check your own guidebook rather than treating a different number as a printing mistake.

Readers commonly use the Major Arcana to explore larger themes or turning points. That is an interpretive approach, not evidence that drawing one of these cards predicts a major event. The [Fool guide](/blog/the-fool-tarot-card-meaning) and [Lovers guide](/blog/the-lovers-tarot-card-meaning) show how a broad theme can become a practical reflection.

## The 56 Minor Arcana cards

The Minor Arcana consists of four suits with 14 cards in each: ten numbered cards and four court cards. The numbered sequence starts with the Ace, followed by 2 through 10. Counting the Ace as one numbered card prevents a common mistake when checking a deck.

Each suit then adds Page, Knight, Queen and King. That makes 10 + 4 = 14 per suit, and 4 × 14 = 56 across the Minor Arcana. Add the 22 Major Arcana cards and you reach 78.

Modern guidebooks often connect the Minor Arcana with everyday situations. A suit can give a reader a starting theme, while the number, image and spread position help shape the question. A single keyword does not describe every possible reading.

## What are the four tarot suits?

Wands is commonly connected with action, initiative and creative energy. Cups often concerns emotions and relationships. Swords is associated with thought, communication and conflict. Pentacles commonly relates to material life, work and resources.

These associations belong to tarot traditions. They do not diagnose a person's health or predict what will happen to their finances. Use them as an organising language for reflection.

You may see Coins or Disks instead of Pentacles, and Batons or Staves instead of Wands. A renamed suit does not automatically change the number of cards. Some modern decks also rename the court roles to make the characters fit their artwork or approach.

The [tarot card meanings hub](/tarot-card-meanings) is the place to explore individual cards after you understand the structure. This guide explains how the deck is counted; the hub helps you look up what a particular card can mean.

## Are court cards included in the 78?

Yes. The 16 court cards are part of the 56 Minor Arcana, not an extra group added afterwards. There are four court cards in each of four suits.

A Queen or King does not have to represent a woman or man in your life. Readers may treat a court card as an approach, a role or a quality to consider. For an example, the [Queen of Wands guide](/blog/queen-of-wands-tarot-card-meaning) explores confidence and participation without using the card to identify a specific person.

## Why your tarot box might contain extra cards

Lay out the Major Arcana, then sort each suit from Ace to King. Put any unnumbered bonus or publisher cards to one side. Compare the results with the booklet's contents list before deciding whether a card is missing.

An extra card can be an intentional part of a particular artist's deck. You can use it as the guidebook suggests or set it aside while learning the standard system. Neither choice makes a reading more accurate or more spiritually legitimate.

A deck containing only the Major Arcana has 22 cards and can be used on its own, but it is not the full standard 78-card deck. Oracle decks follow their own systems and have no single fixed card count. Their booklet is the best starting point for that specific deck.

## Learn the deck without memorising everything

Start by recognising whether a card belongs to the Major or Minor Arcana. For a Minor card, identify its suit and rank. Then describe what you can see in the picture before checking a keyword.

If you want a low-pressure practice, use the [free tarot draw](/free-tarot) and write one question the image brings to mind. You do not need to memorise all 78 meanings before making a useful observation. Keep real decisions grounded in evidence, particularly when they affect health, money or another person's consent.

## Common questions

**Does the Fool count as a card?**

Yes. In many decks it is numbered 0, but it counts as one of the 22 Major Arcana cards.

**Does every tarot deck have 78 cards?**

The standard modern structure does. Some historical or specialist decks differ, and some publishers add bonus cards. Check the deck's own contents list.

**Is a tarot deck the same as a 52-card playing deck?**

No. A standard tarot deck has an additional major group and four courts per suit. An ordinary playing deck has a different structure.
