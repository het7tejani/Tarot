The Wheel of Fortune is card number 10 of the Major Arcana, and it arrives with a simple message people rarely enjoy hearing: things are turning, whether or not you feel ready. It is the tarot's card of cycles. Whatever is down comes up, whatever is up comes down, and the only real question the card asks is what you will do with the turn.

A tarot card cannot see the future. It gives you an image to think with, and this one asks where you are on the wheel right now, and whether you are fighting the rotation or moving with it.

## What the Wheel of Fortune shows

In the Rider-Waite deck, a great wheel hangs in the sky among clouds. The wheel carries two sets of letters, TARO in one direction and the four Hebrew letters of the divine name in the other, plus alchemical symbols for mercury, sulphur, salt and water. On top of the wheel sits a sphinx holding a sword, steady while everything beneath it moves. A serpent slides down the left side, the old Egyptian symbol of the descending force, while a red jackal-headed figure rises on the right, the ascending one. In the four corners, four winged creatures, an angel, an eagle, a lion and a bull, each sit reading a book. They represent the four fixed signs of the zodiac, and the books say that understanding does not move even when circumstances do.

Pamela Colman Smith drew this scene in 1909, and the composition makes the card's argument before any keyword list does: everything turns, except the part of you that watches and learns.

In the Golden Dawn astrology mapping, the Wheel of Fortune belongs to Jupiter, the planet of growth, expansion and opportunity.

## The Wheel of Fortune upright

Upright, the Wheel marks a turning point. A stalled situation starts moving. An effort that looked wasted begins to pay. Readers often describe it as luck improving, but the more useful reading is timing: the cycle you are in has reached its bend, and conditions that were against you start working for you.

The card also carries a reminder about the top of the wheel. If things are going well, the Wheel asks you to use the good stretch deliberately rather than coast, because wheels do not stop at the top either. Save the money, send the applications, have the conversation. Good timing is a resource, and it expires.

## The Wheel of Fortune reversed

Reversed, the Wheel usually describes resistance to a change that is already underway, or a run of bad timing: plans delayed, doors closing, the feeling of pushing a wheel that pushes back. The card's advice is blunt. Stop pushing in the same direction. A reversed Wheel often means the timing is wrong for this approach, not wrong forever.

There is a second reversed reading worth knowing: the cycle is repeating because the lesson was skipped. The same job problem with a new employer, the same argument with a new partner. When the reversed Wheel appears, many readers ask what keeps coming back, because that is where the wheel is catching.

## The Wheel in love readings

In love readings, the Wheel upright often marks a relationship entering a new phase: a meeting that turns into something serious, a couple moving in together, a single stretch finally ending. It suggests the change is bigger than either person, so the advice is to let it develop rather than force a timeline on it.

Reversed in love, the card can describe an on-off pattern or a relationship stuck in a loop of the same fight on rotation. The way off a repeating wheel is never one more lap. ## The Wheel in career and money readings

At work, the Wheel upright is one of the better cards for change: a restructure that opens a door, a market shifting in your favour, a role appearing at the moment you are ready for it. It favours people who have prepared during the slow stretch, because the turn rewards readiness more than it rewards wishing.

In money readings, the card points at fluctuation. Income that rises and falls, an expense cycle, an investment moving. Upright, readers usually advise riding the upswing while building a buffer for the next downswing. Reversed, it asks you not to make permanent financial decisions at the bottom of a temporary dip. ## What to do when the Wheel appears

Locate yourself on the cycle honestly. If things are rising, act: this is the stretch where effort compounds. If things are falling, hold steady and learn: this is the stretch where judgment forms. The one thing the Wheel never recommends is standing still and complaining about the motion, because the motion is the one constant in the picture.

The sphinx on top of the wheel is the model. It holds the sword, it stays put, and it watches the whole rotation without being thrown by it.

## Questions about the Wheel of Fortune

**Is the Wheel of Fortune a good card?**
It is a truthful one. Upright it usually signals a favourable turn, but the card's real promise is movement, not comfort. What was stuck will not stay stuck.

**Does it mean good luck is coming?**
Readers connect it with improving luck, especially upright, but it describes timing more than fortune. The practical reading is that conditions are shifting, and prepared people benefit most from a shift.

**What does the Wheel of Fortune mean in a yes or no reading?**
Upright, most readers lean yes, with the note that timing matters. Reversed, they lean toward not yet rather than no.

**Which zodiac sign is the Wheel of Fortune linked to?**
It belongs to Jupiter rather than a single sign, in the Golden Dawn mapping. The four creatures in its corners represent the four fixed signs: Aquarius, Scorpio, Leo and Taurus.
